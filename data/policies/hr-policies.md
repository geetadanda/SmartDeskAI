# HR Policies

**SmartHR Solutions | Employee Handbook Draft | San Ramon, California**

> **Draft for review:** These proposed policies are not a substitute for current federal, California, or local law, benefit-plan documents, or individual employment agreements. HR and employment counsel should review before publication, particularly leave, accommodations, harassment, and pay-related procedures.

## 1. Leave policy

### 1.1 General rules and eligibility

This policy applies to employees of SmartHR Solutions. Company leave benefits are available from the first day of employment, subject to any legal eligibility rules for a particular leave. Annual leave entitlements are based on the calendar year and are prorated for employees who join during the year or work reduced schedules. A leave day means the employee's normally scheduled workday; leave is charged only for scheduled work time missed.

Where applicable law provides greater rights or benefits, the law controls. HR will coordinate company and statutory leave as permitted by law. Employees will not be retaliated against for requesting or using protected leave.

### 1.2 Casual leave — 12 days per year

Employees receive 12 paid casual-leave days per calendar year, prorated for a partial year or reduced schedule. Casual leave is for personal matters not covered by another leave category.

Request planned time through the designated leave system, normally at least three business days in advance. For an unexpected absence, notify the manager as soon as practicable and record the leave when able. Managers should respond promptly and consider coverage consistently. Unused casual leave does not carry forward or get paid out at year-end or separation, except where required by law.

### 1.3 Sick leave — 8 days per year

Employees receive eight paid sick-leave days per calendar year, prorated for a partial year or reduced schedule. Sick leave may be used for the employee's illness, diagnosis, treatment, or preventive care and for other purposes protected by applicable law, including qualifying care for a family member or designated person. The company will provide any greater amount or use rights required by law.

Notify the manager as soon as practicable. For foreseeable appointments, provide advance notice when possible. HR may request documentation only when permitted by law and reasonably necessary to administer the leave; employees do not need to disclose a diagnosis to their manager. Sick leave does not carry forward or get paid out under this policy, except where applicable law requires otherwise.

### 1.4 Earned leave — 15 days per year

Employees accrue 15 paid earned-leave days per calendar year (equivalent to 1.25 days per month for a full-time employee), prorated for a partial year or reduced schedule. Accrued earned leave is available as it accrues and is not forfeited.

Submit planned leave normally at least five business days in advance; submit longer absences as early as practicable. Managers consider staffing and business needs consistently, respond promptly, and discuss alternatives if a request cannot be approved as submitted.

Unused accrued earned leave carries forward. Accrual may pause when a balance reaches 30 days and resume when it falls below 30 days, subject to applicable law. Accrued, unused earned leave is paid upon separation at the employee's final rate of pay as required by California law. It is not otherwise encashed during employment unless the company establishes a legally compliant program.

### 1.5 Maternity leave — 26 weeks

An employee who gives birth may take up to 26 weeks of fully paid company maternity leave for pregnancy, childbirth, recovery, and bonding. This company benefit is available from the first day of employment and pays regular base pay during the approved leave. Employees are not required to use accrued PTO to fund this benefit.

When foreseeable, notify HR and the manager as early as practicable, normally at least 30 days before leave is expected to begin. If that is not possible, provide notice as soon as practicable. HR will coordinate scheduling and request medical information only as permitted by law and necessary to administer leave or an accommodation. Health coverage, reinstatement, pregnancy-disability rights, and other statutory rights are administered under applicable law and benefit-plan terms; this company benefit does not reduce those rights.

### 1.6 Paternity and parental bonding leave — 2 weeks

An eligible employee who becomes a parent and is not taking the maternity benefit above may take up to two weeks of fully paid company paternity/parental-bonding leave. It is available from the first day of employment for the birth, adoption, or foster placement of a child, and should normally be used within 12 months of the event.

Leave may be taken continuously or in separate blocks with HR approval, subject to applicable law. Give HR and the manager as much advance notice as practicable, normally 30 days when foreseeable. This benefit does not replace or reduce statutory family or bonding leave, benefits, or job-protection rights.

### 1.7 Compensatory time off

Compensatory time off (“comp off”) may be approved when an employee performs additional work on a scheduled day off, company holiday, or outside the employee's normal schedule, where the additional time does not need to be paid as overtime under applicable law.

Obtain manager approval before performing additional work whenever practicable. The manager records the hours and reason. Request use normally at least three business days in advance. Comp off should generally be used within 60 calendar days of being earned and does not carry beyond that period or get encashed, except where required by law. Non-exempt employees must record all hours worked and receive legally required wages and overtime; comp off never replaces required pay.

### 1.8 Request and return process

Submit requests in the designated system. If it is unavailable, contact the manager and HR directly, then record the leave when possible. Give notice consistent with the relevant section; emergencies and unexpected illness should be reported as soon as practicable. Notify HR and the manager promptly about changed dates or an extension. HR will administer confidentiality, documentation, benefits continuation, and return-to-work requirements in accordance with law.

## 2. Work-from-home (WFH) guidelines

WFH is a work arrangement, not a separate leave benefit or a change to an employee's job duties. Employees may work remotely only when the role and manager-approved arrangement allow it. Managers apply eligibility and scheduling standards consistently and may adjust an arrangement for business, performance, security, or operational reasons, subject to accommodation and other legal obligations.

Employees working remotely must:

- Be available during agreed working hours, attend required meetings, and keep their calendar and location information current.
- Use approved devices, network access, MFA, and company collaboration tools; follow IT security requirements.
- Protect confidential information from household members and the public; use a private workspace for sensitive discussions.
- Maintain a safe, functional workspace and promptly report work-related injuries or equipment problems through the normal process.
- Obtain manager approval before working from another state or country. Location changes may affect payroll, tax, employment-law, benefits, and security obligations.

The company will provide or reimburse equipment and expenses according to approved equipment and reimbursement procedures. WFH does not authorize employees to work while on leave or outside recorded hours. Non-exempt employees must record all time worked and obtain approval for overtime when practicable; all hours worked must still be reported.

## 3. Business expense reimbursement

SmartHR Solutions reimburses reasonable, necessary, work-related expenses that are authorized under company rules and applicable law. Personal expenses, fines, ordinary commuting costs, and expenses already paid by the company are not reimbursable unless law or a written approval requires otherwise.

Before incurring a non-routine, high-cost, travel, or client-related expense, obtain written manager approval and follow any procurement or travel requirements. Submit an itemized receipt and business purpose through the designated expense process within 30 calendar days, or as soon as practicable if an exception applies. Include the date, amount, vendor, currency, attendees or project when relevant, and any required allocation.

Managers verify business purpose and budget authority; Finance checks documentation and policy compliance. The company will reimburse approved expenses promptly and in accordance with applicable law. If a submission is incomplete, Finance will explain what is needed. Employees must not split purchases to avoid approval limits or claim the same expense twice. Report a suspected duplicate, incorrect reimbursement, or fraud concern to Finance or HR.

## 4. Code of conduct

Employees are expected to act honestly, respectfully, and lawfully; perform their duties responsibly; protect confidential and personal information; and use company resources for authorized purposes. Employees must disclose actual or potential conflicts of interest, including outside work or financial interests that could affect company decisions, to their manager or HR.

Employees must not falsify records, misuse company funds or access, offer or accept improper benefits, retaliate against someone raising a concern, or use company systems to harass, discriminate, threaten, or unlawfully monitor others. Business records must be accurate and completed promptly. Confidential information may be accessed or shared only for a legitimate work purpose and with authorized recipients.

Raise concerns in good faith with a manager, HR, or the designated reporting channel. The company will review concerns fairly, limit information sharing to those who need it, and prohibit retaliation for good-faith reports or participation in an investigation. Policy violations may result in corrective action consistent with applicable law and company procedures.

## 5. Anti-harassment and equal opportunity

SmartHR Solutions prohibits unlawful discrimination, harassment, and retaliation. This applies to conduct based on any characteristic protected by applicable law and to conduct by or toward employees, applicants, interns, contractors, customers, vendors, or visitors in work-related settings, including remote work and work events.

Prohibited conduct may be verbal, physical, visual, or digital. Examples include slurs, unwelcome sexual advances, requests for sexual favors, demeaning or threatening comments, offensive images, repeated unwanted contact, and retaliation for rejecting or reporting misconduct. This list is illustrative, not exhaustive.

Anyone who experiences or witnesses possible misconduct should report it promptly to a manager, HR, or the designated reporting channel. A report may be made even if the person is unsure whether the conduct violates policy. Managers who receive a concern must forward it promptly to HR and must not promise absolute confidentiality or conduct their own investigation.

HR will assess and investigate reports promptly and impartially, take appropriate interim steps where warranted, protect privacy to the extent practicable, and communicate the outcome as appropriate. Retaliation is prohibited and should be reported immediately. Employees may also contact applicable government agencies; internal reporting is not a prerequisite to exercising legal rights.

## 6. Performance review cycle

Performance management is an ongoing process intended to clarify expectations, recognize contributions, and support development. Managers should provide regular feedback rather than waiting for a formal review.

The standard cycle consists of:

1. **Goal setting:** At the start of the review period, the employee and manager agree on role expectations, measurable goals, and development priorities.
2. **Check-ins:** Manager and employee discuss progress, workload, feedback, and changes to goals during the year. Goals may be updated when business priorities change.
3. **Mid-cycle conversation:** The manager and employee review progress, identify support needed, and document significant changes.
4. **Annual review:** The manager summarizes contributions, outcomes, strengths, and development areas using role-relevant evidence. The employee may provide a self-assessment and written comments.
5. **Follow-up:** Manager and employee agree on next-period goals and any development actions. HR may calibrate reviews to support consistency.

Reviews inform, but do not guarantee, decisions about compensation, promotion, or continued employment. Employees may discuss factual errors or process concerns with the manager or HR. Performance records are handled as confidential employment information, subject to applicable rights and law.
