# Onboarding Policies

**SmartHR Solutions | Employee Handbook Draft | San Ramon, California**

> **Draft for review:** HR and IT should insert current system links, named contacts, enrollment deadlines, and role-specific requirements before publication. Benefits-plan documents and applicable law control if they differ from this summary.

## 1. Purpose and scope

SmartHR Solutions onboarding helps new employees understand their role, access the tools and benefits needed for work, and connect with their team. Managers, HR, IT, and the employee share responsibility for completing onboarding steps. Requirements may vary by role, location, and employment arrangement.

## 2. Day-one checklist

### Before the first day

- **HR:** Confirm offer and employment records, work location, start date, required forms, policy acknowledgments, and benefits-enrollment instructions.
- **Manager:** Prepare the first-week schedule, role expectations, initial goals, introductions, and any role-specific training.
- **IT:** Provision approved accounts, access, equipment, and security enrollment based on the employee's role and least-privilege requirements.
- **Employee:** Complete requested employment and payroll forms through approved channels; provide only requested information and ask HR if a form or deadline is unclear.

### On the first day

1. Meet with HR or the designated onboarding contact for an orientation to company policies, reporting channels, timekeeping, and benefits resources.
2. Meet with the manager to review responsibilities, team practices, first-week priorities, communication norms, and how performance feedback works.
3. Confirm access to email, collaboration tools, required business applications, MFA, and the service desk. Report access problems through IT.
4. Review information security, privacy, acceptable-use, safety, and any role-specific compliance requirements.
5. Confirm the employee's work schedule, location expectations, break and time-recording procedures, and whom to contact for help.

The manager should check in at the end of the first week and periodically during the introductory period. Required onboarding training must be completed by its stated due date.

## 3. IT equipment allocation

Company equipment is assigned according to role, approved budget, availability, and security requirements. The manager submits the equipment request before the start date; IT configures and records issued items. Equipment may include a computer, accessories, security key, or other approved tools.

Employees must:

- Use company devices primarily for authorized work and follow IT security and acceptable-use requirements.
- Keep devices physically secure, apply updates, use required screen locks and MFA, and avoid sharing devices or credentials.
- Promptly report loss, theft, damage, malfunction, or suspected compromise to IT and the manager.
- Obtain written IT approval before adding hardware, modifying security settings, or installing software outside approved processes.
- Return equipment, accessories, and other company property when requested or when employment ends, following IT's instructions.

IT will explain any device-management controls and may update, repair, replace, lock, or remove company data from a device when necessary to protect company systems, subject to applicable law and any applicable agreement. Employees should not store personal data on company devices where practicable.

## 4. Benefits enrollment

HR provides the available benefit options, plan summaries, eligibility information, enrollment instructions, and applicable deadlines. Employees should review official plan documents carefully; those documents control coverage, exclusions, costs, and claims.

New employees must submit elections or waivers through the designated benefits portal within the enrollment window communicated by HR. The window may depend on the plan and applicable law. Employees should not assume coverage is active until they receive confirmation. HR can explain enrollment steps but cannot guarantee a particular insurer's coverage decision.

Employees are responsible for reviewing dependent information, beneficiary designations, payroll deductions, and confirmation statements. Report a qualifying life event or a change to personal information promptly to HR and follow the plan's deadlines. If a deadline is missed or an enrollment record appears incorrect, contact HR immediately; corrections are subject to plan terms and law.

## 5. Buddy programme

Where available, HR or the manager pairs a new employee with a volunteer colleague for informal support, typically during the first month. A buddy can help the employee navigate team norms, find resources, and identify the right contact for common questions.

The buddy is not a manager, HR representative, or substitute for formal training. Employees should raise employment, accommodation, payroll, benefits, safety, or confidential concerns directly with HR or the appropriate company channel. Participation is intended to be supportive and does not affect performance ratings.

## 6. Probationary / introductory period

The standard introductory period is the first 90 calendar days of employment. During this time, the employee and manager review role expectations, training completion, work quality, collaboration, and support needs. The manager should provide feedback during the period and hold a documented check-in near its conclusion.

The introductory period is not a guarantee of continued employment, a promise of employment for a fixed term, or a waiver of any legal rights. It does not delay benefits, leave, or other eligibility unless a specific plan or applicable law provides otherwise. Any extension or change must be discussed with HR and documented. At-will status, where applicable, is governed by the employee's signed agreement and applicable law, not this summary.

If performance or role expectations are unclear, employees should raise the concern with their manager or HR promptly. The company may provide coaching, training, or other appropriate support.
